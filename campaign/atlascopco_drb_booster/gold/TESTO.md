[p10.b2] 1.2 Description
[p10.b3] 1.2.1 Overview
[p10.b4] The Atlas Copco DRB pumps are rotary lobe vacuum booster pumps which in connection with suitable backing pumps are capable of pumping gases and vapors. They are employed to increase the pumping speed of backing pumps below 10-100 mbar by a very significant factor or for the purpose of attaining a lower ultimate pressure. The DRB pumps can pump gas in the vertical or horizontal direction. The DRB pumps use mineral oil Tigol 100. The Standard DRB pumps are not suited for pumping of oxygen when the oxygen concentration exceeds that in the atmosphere. All of the DRB pumps have ISO-F inlet-flange and outlet-flange connections

[p12.b2] 1.2.2 General construction
[p12.b3.1] The DRB pumps are driven directly by an electric motor. They have a pressure balance line between the discharge and intake flange (see Section 1.2.4). The DRB pumps are air-cooled. The airflow for cooling the motor and pump is produced by a fan which sits on the motor. An additional fan is located on the coupling for additional cooling. The pump shafts and rotors are made of forged steel. The internal and external shaft-seals are made of polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE). Although the pumping chamber of DRB pump is free of sealing agents and lubricants, the two gearwheels of the synchromesh gearing and the bearings are lubricated with mineral oil (see Figure 1).
[p12.b3.2] The gearwheels and bearings of the DRB are located in two side chambers which also contain the oil supply. These two side chambers are separated from the pumping chamber by the rotor seals. During operation of the pump, the side chambers are evacuated via the rotor seals. The side chambers are linked to each other by two passages. These passages are arranged so that for either horizontal or vertical flow the pressure will be equalized between the oil supplies. In both side chambers there are integrated oil pumps to ensure that the bearings and gearwheels receive sufficient lubricant at all recommended speeds. The motor of the DRB pump is directly flanged to the coupling housing.
[p12.b3.3] One shaft of the pump is linked to the shaft of the motor by an elastic coupling. The shaft of the other rotor is driven via the synchromesh gear. With the standard motors, the DRB pump can run on either 50 Hz or 60 Hz power supplies. The speed is then increased to 3,600 rpm at 60 Hz and the pumping speed increases correspondingly. The feedthrough of the rotor’s shaft between the evacuated bearing space and the atmosphere is sealed by means of shaft seals. The shaft seals are immersed in oil. They are located in a seal housing with a separate oil reservoir. The oil level in the shaft seal housing can be checked at oiler.
[p12.b4] 1.2.3 Principle of operation
[p12.b5] The DRB pumps - also known as Roots blowers or rotary lobe boosters - contain in their pump casing two symmetrical rotors/ lobes rotating in opposite directions (see Figure 2). The rotors have roughly the cross section of a figure “8” and are synchronized by a toothed gear so that they move past each other and the casing without contact but with a small clearance. The DRB pumps have been specifically designed for operation in the rough and fine vacuum ranges. They are thus either used in connection with backing pumps or in closed gas cycles.
[p12.b6] 1.2.4 Pressure balance line
[p12.b7.1] The DRB pump has an integrated pressure balance line (see Figure 2, item 6). It links the discharge and intake flanges via a pressure balance valve. If the pressure differential between the flanges is too large, the valve opens. Some of the gas which has already been pumped then flows back through the line to the intake flange. The valve is weight- and spring-loaded so that it works with both vertical and horizontal flow of the pump. In the case of 50/60 Hz operation and due to the pressure balance line, no additional controlling equipment will be needed to protect the pump against pressure differences which are too high.
[p12.b7.2] The DRB pump can then be switched on together with a backing pump at atmospheric pressure. Thus the pumping speed of the pump combination is increased also at high intake pressures. The pressure balance valve will not protect the pump from thermal overload if opened continuously.

[p13.b2] 1.2.5 Non-conforming Utilization
[p13.b3] WARNING The non-conforming utilization of pump and accessories may result in severe injury or damage to the components. Non-conforming utilizations for the pump are among others: 1 Pumping of gases and vapors for which the materials of the pump are not suited. 2 Pumping of condensable vapors without adequately controlling the temperature of the [p13.b4] pump. [p29.b5] 5.4 Changing from Vertical to Horizontal Flow
[p29.b6.1] The DRB pumps are supplied as standard for vertical flow unless you specifically request horizontal flow. Moreover, the pump may be converted from one flow direction to the other. For this proceed as follows: Unscrew the sealing screws and drain out the oil from the side chambers (see Figure 11). Drain the oil from the oiler (see Section 6.4). Seal off of the bottom opening with the closure screw and a seal which is in perfect condition so that a vacuum-tight seal is attained again. Unscrew the pump’s feet, turn the pump by 90° and as shown in Table 3 (dimensional data) fit the feet for the changed direction of pumping action.
[p29.b6.2] The longitudinal axis of the pump must remain horizontal so that no residual lubricant can flow from the side chambers into the pumping chamber. Unscrew oiler and sealing screw. Screw in the oiler from the top using a perfect gasket and screw in the sealing screw from the side also using a perfect gasket. Fill in the oil for the side chambers at oil-fill opening and the oil for the shaft seal housing at the oiler. If a pressure switch has been installed, turn it so that it again points vertically upwards. The valve in the pressure balance line of the DRB is designed to work with both vertical and horizontal flow of the pump.
